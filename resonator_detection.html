Model conventions

Sideband quadratures use shot noise = 1. Spectral quadratures are p(Ω)=(Xs+iYa)/√2 and q(Ω)=(Ys−iXa)/√2.

r(Δ) = 1 − ηc / (1/2 − iΔ),   ηc = κex/κ S(Δ) = 1 + Sp contribution + Sq contribution + Cpq contribution + hidden contribution

The carrier sees r(Δ), the upper sideband r(Δ+Ω), and the lower sideband r(Δ−Ω). Internal loss injects vacuum and pulls the measured noise toward 1 near resonance.

How to read the curve
  • Far from resonance: S → Sp; direct detection sees amplitude noise.
  • Near Δ≈±Ω: one sideband is resonant, converting phase and correlation information into amplitude noise.
  • Near Δ≈0: the carrier phase rotates relative to off-resonant sidebands; for Ω/κ≫1 this exposes Sq.
  • Cpq skews the lobes; upper/lower sideband imbalance breaks Δ→−Δ symmetry.

Reference framing follows Barbosa et al., PRL 111, 200402 (2013) and Villar, arXiv:0805.0528, as in the notebook.
